# Justice in Data: Engineering & Data Justice

A week-long or self-paced bootcamp for undergraduate researchers integrating FAIR data principles and machine learning techniques (Python) within an engineering research justice context.

## Module Overview

**Presenters:** June Young Park, PhD (Assistant Professor) and Jessica Eisma, PhD (Assistant Professor). **Source materials:** Engineering_Justice_Final.pdf, Engineering Justice Recording (YouTube video X_KOxVqCVgs), Engineering Justice Pre-Survey.

## Social Justice and Engineering Frameworks

Social justice defined as equitable distribution of wealth, opportunities, and privileges. Seven-step engineering justice framework (from Leydens & Lucena, 2017, "Engineering Justice: Transforming Engineering Education and Practice"): (1) listening to communities, (2) identifying structural conditions, (3) increasing human rights, (4) increasing opportunities, (5) increasing resources, (6) reducing imposed risks and harms, (7) enhancing human capabilities. Framework applies universally across all engineering domains and industries.

## Energy and Water Equity Issues

**Building Energy Insecurity:** US Energy Information Administration's 2015 Residential Energy Consumption Survey data shows 30%+ households reported energy insecurity; 20%+ reduced or forgone basic necessities to pay energy bills; 15% received disconnection notices; 10%+ maintained unsafe/unhealthy home temperatures due to affordability. Engineers influence through professional advocacy and organizational pressure.

**Sanitation and Safe Drinking Water:** 2.4 billion people globally lack basic sanitation (World's Largest Youth Water Organization). In the US, 2+ million Americans lack safe drinking water/sanitation access. UN designates water access as universal human right. **Water Pricing Crisis:** Circle of Blue study found 54% water rate increases across US since 2010 (data from 2017); approximately one-third of Americans may be unable to pay monthly water bills.

## Climate and Infrastructure Inequities

**Extreme Weather in Built Environment:** Climate change drives more severe temperature extremes, floods, and droughts. **Floods and Droughts:** Second deadliest US weather hazard (~98 deaths/year from drowning); drought causes wildfires, dust storms, extreme heat, flash flooding, degraded water quality. Last two decades: 3 billion people affected by floods/droughts with catastrophic human and economic costs. Poor and marginalized communities experience first and worst impacts.

**Occupant Comfort Inequity:** Historical comfort studies favored male temperature preferences (cooler); office buildings optimized for males, creating suboptimal conditions for female occupants, potentially reducing performance. **Disability-Related Inequity:** American with Disabilities Act establishes standards for accessible infrastructure, but people with disabilities report designs don't match standards or actual accessibility needs. Universal Design approach requires listening to individuals with disabilities.

## Research Inequality

**Structural University Stratification:** US universities show increasing inequality; 80-90% of financial resources concentrated in top 10-20% institutions. Elite universities monopolize equipment, training, and personnel support, creating barriers for less-resourced institutions. Inequality in research funding deepens over time: inequality between public and private universities initially declined but now increasing, concentrating federal grants on elite institutions.

**Racial Inequality in Funding:** 2019 NSF proposal data shows white researchers funded at rates higher than proportional representation; Asian, Hispanic, Black, American Indian/Alaska Native, and Pacific Islander researchers funded below proportional rates. Gender disparities also exist.

## FAIR Data Principles Solution

Address research inequalities through accessible research practices: FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data principles. Goal: make research data and processes easily accessible to researchers worldwide, enabling equitable participation and diverse insights across science domains regardless of institutional resources.
