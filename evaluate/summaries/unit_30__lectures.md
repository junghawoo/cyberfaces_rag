# Lectures

Foundation-level lecture module on climate change impacts, food security, and energy policy by Matthew Huber (Purdue University, Climate Dynamics Prediction Lab), supported by National Science Foundation, Purdue University, and Mellon Foundation.

**Climate Change: Food Security Lecture Content:**

**Temperature & Atmospheric Trends:** Global combined land-ocean surface temperatures rising steadily since 1850; each past three decades warmer than preceding decades (IPCC AR5 Synthesis Report). Ocean heat content increasing. Global CO2 concentration increased from 277 ppm (1750) to 397 ppm (2014), with first seasonally-corrected monthly mean exceeding 400 ppm (March 2015, Mauna Loa). Without mitigation, global mean surface temperature may increase 3.7-4.8°C over 21st century. Current emission pledges (INDCs) from top-4 emitters (US, EU, China, India) exceed 2°C emission budget (66% chance). Actual CO2 emissions rising again: China and India rely heavily on coal; US and EU slowly decarbonizing. Projected 37.1 Gt global emissions (2018, Figueres et al. 2018).

**Climate Sensitivity & Models:** Climate sensitivity ranges 1.5-4.5°C per doubling CO2 with peak ~2-3°C and long tail toward higher values (Meinshausen et al. 2009). Paleoclimate data (PALAEOSENS 2012) and CMIP5 models indicate variability. Community Earth System Model (CESM) version 4 from NCAR simulates Earth's climate system. Global circulation models: CCSM/NCAR, GFDL/NOAA, HadCM3/UK, PCM/NCAR. RCP scenarios (2.6, 4.5, 6.0, 8.5) project temperature changes 2046-2065, 2081-2100, 2181-2200, 2281-2300.

**Food Security Impacts:** Food security increasing but climate poses significant threats. 2012 US heatwave threatened grain harvest; soybean futures rose to $15.42/bushel (highest since July 2008), corn to near $7/bushel; Illinois and Indiana exceeded 38°C (100°F). May 2015 India heat wave killed 17+ million broiler chickens, chicken prices soared to record 95 rupees/kg, poultry feed demand dropped 30% in 2-3 weeks. Climate impacts on agriculture include yield variability, pest/disease pressure, water stress.

**Planetary Boundaries Framework (Steffen et al. 2015):** Nine critical processes regulating Earth system functioning; climate change and biosphere integrity identified as core boundaries with potential to drive system into new state. Holocene epoch (11,700 years) represents only stable state supporting contemporary human societies. IPCC AR5 emphasizes irreversibility and long-term climate commitments.

**Biofuel Policy Case Study:** "Use of U.S. Croplands for Biofuels Increases Greenhouse Gases Through Emissions from Land-Use Change" (Searchinger et al.): Corn-based ethanol produces nearly double greenhouse gas emissions over 30 years and increases emissions for 167 years, contrary to prior analyses assuming biofuel carbon sequestration. Switchgrass biofuels increase emissions 50% if grown on US corn lands. Land clearing carbon debt: converting rainforests, peatlands, savannas, grasslands creates 17-420x more CO2 than annual greenhouse gas reductions from biofuels. Waste biomass and degraded land biofuels incur minimal carbon debt.

**Key Sources:** NOAA-ESRL, Scripps Institution of Oceanography, Global Carbon Budget 2015-2016, US Global Change Research Program, Nature publications (Hawker et al. 2018, Saksena & Merwade 2015), Science (Schlenker & Roberts 2009).

## Summarized attachments

- **Climate Change: Food Security Lecture** (`lecture_climate_change_huber.pdf`, PDF): Comprehensive lecture by Matthew Huber (Purdue University, Climate Dynamics Prediction Lab) covering global temperature and CO2 trends (IPCC AR5), climate sensitivity (1.5-4.8°C), global circulation models (CESM, GFDL, HadCM3), RCP scenarios, food security threats, planetary boundaries framework, and biofuel policy case studies with implications for agriculture and human labor capacity.
- **On-going Research Linking Agriculture, Nutrition, and Climate** (`lecture_nutrition_Shively.pptx`, presentation): Lecture slides by Gary Shively presenting nutrition basics (Waaler Curve, stunting, wasting), child growth patterns, demographic trends, linkages between agriculture/climate and nutrition, and research using DHS/LSMS data with satellite vegetation indices (NDVI) to assess child growth sensitivity to local agricultural capacity and rainfall in Nepal and Uganda.
