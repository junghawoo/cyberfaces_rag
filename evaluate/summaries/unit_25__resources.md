# Resources

Expert-level compilation of book chapters addressing global food security, land use sustainability, climate change impacts, and agricultural economics from the Springer publication "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet."

**GLASSNET Resources (MyGeoHub):**
Global Land-use And Food Security on a changing planet network addressing UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Featured researcher: Alexandra Hill (UC Berkeley, Assistant Professor Cooperative Extension) studying US agricultural workforce well-being impacts on income/health and business performance (productivity, output quality, profitability, workplace injuries). Hired farmworkers face economic, physical, legal, emotional hardships; research identifies avenues enhancing employee well-being while maintaining business viability. Thematic seminar series "Modelling Extremes: From Climate Change to Biophysical Impacts and Economic Implications" examines extreme weather event frequency increase from rising temperatures, changing precipitation patterns, blocked weather systems. Addresses challenges to agriculture, food production, food security, resource management. Questions: improve understanding of extreme events and coincidence? Expand modeling chain (biophysical processes to socio-economic impacts)? Evaluate extreme weather propagation through global value chains and adaptation options at different scales?

**Overview of Global Land Use, Food Security and the Environment (Hertel & Baldos):**
Framework for long-run sustainability of food and environmental systems integrating demand factors (population, income, biofuels) and supply factors (technological progress, climate change, competing land uses) to predict agricultural land equilibrium. Addresses "grand challenge" of feeding growing population while ensuring environmental sustainability. Analyzes food race between demand growth and yield improvements. Economic response impacts: reduced consumption (nutrition consequences) vs. increased supplies (environmental impacts). Uses demand/supply responsiveness to scarcity. Builds on Hertel's Presidential Address to Agricultural and Applied Economics Association (2011).

**Population and Income as Drivers of Global Change (Hertel & Baldos):**
Historical relationship between population and cropland expansion. Per capita income role in land use (especially non-agricultural forests). Demographic transition concepts and 2050 population projections. Population-consumption pattern interplay across regions (income-driven differences). Dietary upgrading: consumption shift from starchy foods to meats, dairy, processed foods. Income elasticity of food demand (price responsiveness varies by income level). Projections to 2050 via SIMPLE model on food demand and land use change implications.

**Climate Change Impacts in Agriculture (Hertel & Baldos):**
Evidence on climate change consequences for temperature/precipitation and crop production mechanisms: temperature effects on crop development, elevated CO2 impacts on photosynthesis/respiration, water stress, extreme temperature damage, pest/disease pressure. Empirical evidence quantifying historical impacts; SIMPLE model global simulations of climate impacts on land use/food prices. Adaptation responses: irrigation investment, heat/drought-tolerant crop variety development via agricultural research funding. Successful adaptation offers mitigation benefits via improved yields reducing land conversion and land-use-change emissions. Studies: Hawker et al. (2018), Saksena & Merwade (2015), Schlenker & Roberts (2009, PNAS), Lobell et al. (2013-2014), AgMIP/DSSAT models (Rosenzweig et al. 2013).

**Food Security and Nutrition (Hertel & Baldos):**
Feeding nine billion by 2050: food security definition/measurement, distribution of food consumption, household nutrition shortfalls, influences of agricultural productivity/biofuels/climate change. Integrates poverty-growth elasticities, health metrics (stunting reversibility, maternal/child undernutrition, metabolic disorders), food system vulnerability (disease epidemiology, flood impacts, mycotoxin contamination). References: Ahmed et al. (2009), Lobell et al. (2008), climate-food-security linkages, agricultural productivity research (Alston et al. 2009), multi-country income-consumption patterns (Alexandratos & Bruinsma 2012).

**Keywords:** Intensive/extensive supply margins, agricultural land use change, crop yield potential, land-use-change emissions, biophysical economics, resource economics, sustainable growth, food systems resilience, agro-biodiversity, climate-smart agriculture, policy analysis.

**Description:** Book chapters provided as foundation resources for advanced study in global agriculture, climate adaptation, and food security policy.

Featured researcher: Alexandra Hill, UC Berkeley Assistant Professor of Cooperative Extension, studying US agricultural workforce well-being (income, health, productivity, workplace injuries) and effects on agri-food business viability. Thematic seminar series "Modelling Extremes: From Climate Change to Biophysical Impacts and Economic Implications" examines extreme weather frequency, food security impacts, cross-regional shock coincidence, and biophysical-to-socio-economic modelling chains.

- **Overview of Global Land Use, Food Security and the Environment (link)** (Springer link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_1, book chapter): Chapter 1 from "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Hertel & Baldos, 2016). Framework integrating demand factors (population, income, biofuels) and supply factors (technological progress, climate change, competing land uses) to predict agricultural land equilibrium. Addresses food race between demand growth and yield improvements; analyzes economic responses (reduced consumption vs increased supplies) to scarcity. Builds on Hertel's 2011 Presidential Address to the Agricultural and Applied Economics Association.

- **Population and Income as Drivers of Global Change (link)** (Springer link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_2, book chapter): Chapter 2 covering historical population-cropland expansion relationship, per capita income impacts on land use and non-agricultural forests, demographic transition concepts, and 2050 population projections. Analyzes dietary upgrading (starchy foods shifting to meats, dairy, processed foods), income-driven consumption pattern variation across regions, and income elasticity of food demand (price responsiveness by income level). SIMPLE model projections to 2050 on food demand and land use change.

- **Productivity Growth and Yields in the Global Crops Sector (link)** (Springer link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_3, book chapter): Chapter 3 examining crop productivity growth and long-term land use/environmental/food security implications. Compares productivity growth camps (yield-growth skeptics vs total factor productivity optimists), distinguishing between yield measures and economic output per hectare. Addresses R&D investment as key determinant of future productivity; SIMPLE model simulations show yields are not synonymous with productivity in economic models, depending on both TFP growth and production intensification.
