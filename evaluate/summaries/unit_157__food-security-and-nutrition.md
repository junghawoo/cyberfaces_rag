# Food Security and Nutrition

This unit addresses global food security, nutrition outcomes, and malnutrition distribution through economic modeling and policy analysis, drawing from Springer research and GEMPACK tools.

## Food Security and Nutrition (Book Chapter)

**Chapter Reference**: Hertel, T.W., Baldos, U.L.C. (2016). Food Security and Nutrition. In: Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet. Springer, Cham. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_10. Available at: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_10. ISBN: 978-3-319-22661-3 (Print), 978-3-319-22662-0 (Online). Pages 125-140. Publishers: Springer International Publishing Switzerland (Copyright 2016). Supplemental learning resources: https://mygeohub.org/.

**Authors**: Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos (Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN).

**Central Question**: Can we feed nine billion people by 2050? 

**Content Overview**: Reviews food security definitions and measurements, noting limitations of metrics emphasizing average consumption. Examines food accessibility within UN framework for hunger/caloric malnutrition incidence. Shifts analysis from average to distribution of regional food consumption. Uses modified SIMPLE model for household-level nutrition shortfall projections. Analyzes nutritional outcome influences: agricultural productivity trends, biofuels expansion, climate change impacts.

**Key Concepts**: Global malnutrition headcount, malnutrition gap, agricultural productivity and malnutrition, climate change and malnutrition, biofuels and malnutrition.

**Mathematical Framework**: Undernutrition incidence and gap equivalent to Foster, Greer, and Thorbecke (1984) poverty index/gap measures. Uses poverty-growth elasticities linking nutrition measures to per capita dietary energy intake changes (Bourguignon 2003; Lopez & Serven 2006). Dietary upgrading toward healthier diets: caloric content linked to per capita incomes—higher incomes yield fewer calories per food dollar and shifts toward leaner, higher-quality diets.
