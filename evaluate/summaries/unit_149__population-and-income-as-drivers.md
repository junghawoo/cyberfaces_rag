# Population and Income as Drivers of Global Change

This module examines how population growth and per capita income changes drive global land use and food demand, with focus on agricultural economics and climate change impacts. It combines theoretical concepts from demography and microeconomics with modeling exercises analyzing extensification, intensification, and technical change effects across regions.

**Source materials:** Lab 4 presentation `Lab_4_Discussion.pptx` and supplementary files for course AGEC 596/528 "Global Change and the Challenge of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" taught by Uris Baldos (Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University). Textbook chapter "Population and Income as Drivers of Global Change" by Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos published in "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Springer, 2016; ISBN 978-3-319-22662-0). Supplementary resources available at https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change.

**Core concepts:** Historical relationship between population growth and cropland expansion; demographic transition and UN population projections to 2050. Dietary upgrading: transition from starchy foods toward meats, dairy, and processed foods correlated with income levels. Technical change mechanisms: input-neutral technical change (AOCROP parameter) affecting productivity of both land and non-land inputs equally; land-biased technical change (AFLAND) affecting land productivity only; non-land biased technical change (AFNLAND) affecting non-land input productivity. Technical change creates expansion/contraction effects (shift in derived demand for land outside ECROP expression) and substitution effects (shift toward/away from land depending on price within ECROP expression).

**Modeling framework:** SIMPLE model for projections to 2050 incorporating population growth, income growth, and consumption pattern changes. Zero profit conditions in agricultural economics: global crop price equals weighted combination of land rents and non-land input prices based on cost shares. Favorable/positive technical change reduces input use and firm costs, lowering market crop prices. Adverse/negative technical change increases input use and firm costs, raising market crop prices. GEMPACK software (Release 11.0 or later, Trial Edition available) used to run model simulations and analyze scenarios.

**Lab 4 experiments and regional focus:** Global versus local climate change impacts (TFP-Global-10.shf, TFP-MENA-NAM-SAsia-10.shf, TFP-SSA-10.shf file sets). Role of intensification analysis (NoIntensification.HAR scenario). Land-disaugmenting climate change experiments (AFLAND-MENA-NAM-SAsia-10.shf). 2050 climate change projections (Baseline.SHF, Baseline+cc.SHF). Regional analysis: MENA (Middle East/North Africa), NAM (North America), SAsia (South Asia), SSA (Sub-Saharan Africa), with S_S_Africa sub-regional focus.

**Key learning objectives:** Analyze extensification patterns across scenarios by region; explain crop price dynamics under different technical change scenarios; assess returns to land across regions; evaluate intensification role in constraining prices and land use changes; compare price effects of land-biased versus total factor productivity shocks; interpret land price responses to biased technical change; synthesize multiple model outputs (PCROP-price of crops, QCROPW-quantity crops worldwide, YIELDW-yield worldwide, QLANDW-quantity land worldwide) comparing with/without climate change impacts.

**Data and references:** FAO food security statistics, FAOSTAT database, World Bank development indicators, International Comparison Program data. References: Bloom (2011) on population dynamics, UN World Population Prospects (2010, 2012 revisions), Muhammad et al. (2011) on food consumption patterns, Reimer & Hertel (2003) on demand estimation for GTAP model.
