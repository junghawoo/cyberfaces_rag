# Resources

Developer-level "Resources" unit collecting book chapters and external links related to global change, agricultural economics, land use, and water/food security. Description: "Book chapters."

Fetched resources (external URLs):

GLASS Resources (link) - mygeohub.org/groups/glass. Landing page for the GLASSNET group ("glassnet") on the MyGeohub platform, themed "Meeting the Global Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) on a Changing Planet with Limited Land and Water Resources." GLASSNET aims to give decision makers data to assess actions affecting environment, economy, and communities. Featured Researcher: Alexandra Hill, Assistant Professor of Cooperative Extension, UC Berkeley, researching the US agricultural/hired-farmworker workforce and worker well-being (income, health, productivity, output quality, profits, workplace injuries) and its effects on agri-food businesses. Featured Event: GLASSNET Thematic Seminar Series, "Modelling Extremes: From Climate Change to Biophysical Impacts and Economic Implications," on extreme weather events, food security, coincident cross-regional shocks, and the biophysical-to-socio-economic modelling chain in the global-to-local-to-global research agenda.

Chapter 4 - link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_4 - "Economic Responses to Scarcity" (pp. 41-56), from the book "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" by Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN (Springer, Cham, 2016; DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_4; Print ISBN 978-3-319-22661-3; Online ISBN 978-3-319-22662-0; eBook package Earth and Environmental Science). Abstract: focuses on endogenous economic responses to scarcity when demand growth outpaces productivity growth, examining three margins - the demand margin (consumer response to higher prices), the extensive margin of supply (cropland area responsiveness), and the intensive margin of supply (substituting nonland inputs for land) - and uses simulations with the SIMPLE model to show total cropland conversion, arguing purely biophysical analyses overstate required cropland conversion. Draws on Hertel and Tyner (2013). Keywords: economic responses to agricultural scarcity, intensive margin of supply, extensive margin of supply, food demand response to price, land-sparing, technological change. Includes an extensive reference list citing Hertel, Tyner, Baldos, Ramankutty, Foley, Searchinger, Tilman, Lambin, Golub, Villoria, and others across sources including Science, PNAS, Nature, Global Food Security, GTAP, FAO/FAOSTAT, World Bank, and USDA/NASS. Supplemental learning resources free at mygeohub.org/courses/global_change.

Chapter 5 - link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5 - "Water, Food and Environmental Security" (pp. 57-67), same book/authors (Hertel and Baldos, Purdue; Springer, Cham, 2016; DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5; same ISBNs and eBook package). Abstract: water scarcity as a key twenty-first-century food/environmental sustainability issue; agriculture is the largest water consumer, irrigated agriculture accounts for 70% of global freshwater withdrawals, increasingly from groundwater; irrigated area is under 20% of cropland but ~40% of production; scope exists for improving water use efficiency; uses SIMPLE model simulations on restrictions to irrigated agriculture and global land-use change. Draws heavily on the OECD report by Hertel and Liu (2014). Keywords: water use in agriculture, irrigation efficiency, water scarcity and food production, groundwater depletion, water and food security. References include Alexandratos and Bruinsma, Rosegrant, Molden, Taheripour, Liu, Wada, Taylor, Döll, Siebert, Mekonnen and Hoekstra, and others across Nature, Science, PNAS, Global Environmental Change, Agricultural Water Management, OECD, and FAO sources. Supplemental learning resources free at mygeohub.org.

Both Springer chapters are subscription/paywalled content (preview only) with pricing tiers (Chapter USD 29.95, eBook USD 39.99, Softcover/Hardcover USD 54.99, Springer+ subscription from $39.99/month), available as PDF/EPUB, with citation export formats .RIS/.ENW/.BIB.
