# Economic Responses to Scarcity

**Reference**: Hertel, T.W. (with contributions from Hertel & Tyner 2013). Economic Responses to Scarcity. In: Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet. Springer, Cham. Pages 41-56.

**Core Focus**: Endogenous economic responses to scarcity when demand growth outpaces productivity growth. Demonstrates biophysical analyses significantly overstate cropland conversion requirements.

## Three Margins of Economic Response

**Demand Margin**: Consumers' price response to higher food prices; demand elasticity reduces quantity demanded as prices increase; reduces agricultural scarcity pressure.

**Extensive Margin (Supply)**: Cropland area responsiveness to higher agricultural returns; captures potential for land supply expansion; elasticity varies by region and economic conditions.

**Intensive Margin (Supply)**: Producers' substitution of non-land inputs (fertilizer, labor, capital) for land in response to increased scarcity; driven by relative input prices and technological options.

## Key Mechanisms

**Ricardian Adjustments**: Land sorting over time improves match between land use and land potential; geographic reallocation of agricultural production reflects comparative advantage.

**Market Integration**: Trade responses mediate land use impacts; markets equilibrate supply-demand imbalances across regions; prices signal scarcity.

**Technological Response**: Yield elasticity to prices; crop producers adjust intensification rates based on economic incentives.

## SIMPLE Model Simulations

Analyzes interaction of demand, extensive, and intensive margins in determining total cropland conversion using SIMPLE agricultural economic model; demonstrates how economic mechanisms offset pure biophysical constraints.

## Core Insight

Economic response mechanisms—price signaling, market equilibration, input substitution, land reallocation—significantly ameliorate cropland conversion pressures from growing food demand compared to purely biophysical analyses that ignore economic adaptability.
