# Overview of Global Land Use, Food Security and the Environment

Foundation module for AGEC 596/528 "Global Change and the Challenge of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet," taught by Thomas Hertel (Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University). The unit contains a 29-slide lecture deck (source file: `Analytical Framework for Analysis of Long Run Land Use Change.pptx`, labeled "Jupyter Notebook" resource) plus two fetched external web resources.

Lecture content: an analytical/theoretical framework for characterizing long-run global supply and demand for agricultural land, and its use to predict impacts of a population shock on commodity prices and land use. Global agriculture is modeled as a single sector producing all food, fiber and fuel from farmland and non-land inputs, with cost-minimizing farmers and zero economic profits (comparative static analysis to 2050). Three exogenous drivers: demand growth (population, income, biofuel/bioenergy mandates), trend yield growth from past R&D investment, and reductions in agricultural land supply (urbanization, national parks). Three margins of economic response: price-sensitive demand response, intensive margin (endogenous yield increases via more non-land inputs like fertilizer — "land sparing" intensification), and extensive margin (cropland area expansion). Key concepts: food demand schedules, demand elasticities from ICP international cross-section data (Mohammed/Muhammad et al. 2011: range 0.86–0.30 across 144 countries in 2005; low-income avg 0.74, high-income 0.43, global avg 0.53), elasticity of substitution between land and non-land inputs (isoquants), derived demand for agricultural land, land rents, and the land supply schedule shifted by urbanization. Empirical evidence cited: 86% of 1961–2005 output growth from yields/cropping intensity; US maize yield elasticity estimates falling from 0.76 (post-WWII) to 0.15; Lubowski's NRI-based land cover study (20-year cropland response to rents ~0.15); research/adoption lags from Alston, Pardey and Ruttan (2008), Alston et al. (2010), Beddow (2011). Slides map concepts to the SIMPLE model: tabfile equations E_QPC (per capita commodity demand), E_CONS, E_PCROP, E_QCONS, E_QLANDg, E_PLANDg; variables AOCROP (technical change), ECROP (substitution elasticity), ELANDg; SIMPLE has three food sectors (crops, livestock, processed foods). Repeated reference: Hertel, T. W. (2011), "The Global Supply and Demand for (Agricultural) Land in 2050: A Perfect Storm in the Making?" (AJAE 93(2)); also Leslie Roberts, "9 Billion?", Science vol. 333, 29 July 2011.

## Summarized attachments

- **Analytical Framework for Analysis of Long Run Land Use Change** (Analytical Framework for Analysis of Long Run Land Use Change.pptx, file): Thomas Hertel's lecture presentation on the theoretical framework for analyzing global agricultural land supply and demand, examining how population shocks, yield growth, and land availability affect commodity prices and land use through three economic margins of response.

- **Overview of Global Land Use, Food Security and the Environment** (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_1, remote resource): Springer book chapter providing comprehensive overview of agricultural land use change drivers, including demand growth, intensification versus extensification trade-offs, and the grand challenge of sustainably feeding a growing global population while protecting the environment.

- **GEMPACK software installation page** (https://www.copsmodels.com/gplapsoft.htm, remote resource): Installation instructions for GEMPACK (General Equilibrium Modelling Package) computational software including the GTAP model solver and supporting tools for dynamic general equilibrium modeling of trade and economic policy.
