# Productivity Growth and Yields in the Global Crops Sector

Springer book chapter examining agricultural productivity growth, yield trends, total factor productivity (TFP), and technological innovation driving food security and land use sustainability.

**Authors:** Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University. **Book:** "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Springer, Cham, 2016), Chapter 3, pp. 27-39. **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_3. **ISBN:** 978-3-319-22661-3 (Print), 978-3-319-22662-0 (Online). **Supplemental resources:** https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change

## Historical Productivity and Future Outlook

**Historical accomplishment:** Despite population and income growth past century, cropland expansion modest and crop prices trended downward due to technological innovation and production intensification. **Explores:** Crop productivity growth implications for long-term land use, environment, and food security.

**Competing perspectives:** Two distinct "camps" debate future productivity: (1) slowing yield growth camp—relatively pessimistic outlook; (2) strong total factor productivity (TFP) growth camp—optimistic grounds for feeding 2050 world. **Reconciliation:** Distinction between different productivity measures clarifies debate. Yields and productivity not synonymous in economic models.

## Key Determinants and Measures

**Critical factor:** Investment in R&D for new crop varieties and cultivation technologies determines future productivity growth. **Productivity measures:** Output per hectare depends both on TFP growth and production intensification—not yield growth alone.

**Yield gaps:** Grassini et al. (2013) distinguish yield advances from plateaus; Lobell et al. (2009) document global crop yield gaps; Licker et al. (2010) examine climate and agricultural management effects on yield gaps; Neumann et al. (2010) provide spatial grain production analysis; Van Ittersum et al. (2013) review yield gap analysis.

## Research Tools and Data Sources

**SIMPLE model:** Computable general equilibrium framework for simulations to 2050, illustrating yields-productivity distinction. **Databases:** FAOSTAT (FAO agricultural statistics), World Bank commodity price data, USDA crop production historical records, UN population prospects.

## Key Research Contributors

Major scholars: Alston, Pardey, Ruttan, Bruinsma, Fischer, Byerlee, Edmeades, Fuglie, Herdt, Ludena, Preckel. **Organizations:** International Food Policy Research Institute (ASTI global assessment), FAO (2050 world food demand), World Bank (agriculture prospects, commodity monitoring), ACIAR (Australian aid). **Related research:** Zhu et al. (2010) on photosynthetic efficiency; Griffith et al. (2004) on R&D productivity; Fulginiti & Perrin (1993) on prices and productivity; Sutch (2014) on hybrid corn biotechnology.

**Keywords:** Agricultural productivity growth, total factor productivity, historical yield trends, crop yield gaps, yield plateaus, feed the world 2050.
