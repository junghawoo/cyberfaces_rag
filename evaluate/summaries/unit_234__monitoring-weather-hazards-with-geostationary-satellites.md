# Monitoring Weather Hazards with Geostationary Satellites

Expert module (FAIR Science in Climate) teaching retrospective and near-real-time monitoring of weather hazards — storms, fires, and aerosols — using geostationary satellite data. Prepared by Mohamed Abdelkader, Jorge Bravo, and Marouane Temimi (Civil, Environmental, and Ocean Engineering, Stevens Institute of Technology; mabdelka@stevens.edu) and Jibin Joseph (School of Civil Engineering, Purdue University). Module steps: introduction to geostationary satellites, data acquisition, data processing and visualization, and case studies, worked through sequential Jupyter Notebooks with accompanying PDF solutions.

**Instructions** (source file `Instructions.pdf`): navigating the notebooks starting with Lecture 1, executing Python cells, modifying parameters, and using PDF solution documents.

**Compilation of links to satellite products available on the web** (link, CIRA/RAMMB Colorado State PDF, "Online_Access_to_Satellite_Products_English"): an extensive catalog of GOES, JPSS, and derived products. GOES imagery: CIRA Geostationary Slider (rammb-slider), CIRA RMTC/RAMSDIS Online, NOAA/NESDIS/STAR, RealEarth (SSEC Wisconsin), DSAT and DSA from INPE Brasil, CIMSS/SSEC, NCEI GIBBS archive; NetCDF digital data via Amazon Cloud (Brian Blaylock goes16 download CGI, Univ. of Utah CHPC), NOAA CLASS, NOAA Open Data Dissemination (NODD), and the Registry of Open Data on AWS. POES/VIIRS: CIRA Polar Slider, JSTAR Mapper, NASA Worldview. Geostationary Lightning Mapper (GLM): STAR, NASA SPoRT, CICS-MD University of Maryland, NWS Virtual Lab, Satellite Liaison blog, HWT Tales from the Testbed. Precipitable water: MIMIC TPW (CIMSS-UW), CIRA AMSU/SSMI, NESDIS Blended TPW, Advected Layered Precipitable Water (ALPW). SST/ocean color: NOAA PSL, OSPO SST and anomalies, NNVL OISST, GHRSST, OSTIA (Met Office), STAR OCView. Hydro-estimators/floods: STAR Satellite Rainfall Estimates, JAXA GSMaP, GPM IMERG viewer, CMORPH, SSEC flood-map demo. Derived Motion Winds (DMW) for GOES-East/West; ASCAT/ScatSat scatterometer winds. Fires/smoke: RealEarth Fire Radiative Power, OSPO fire products, STAR VIIRS Fire & Smoke, ESRL RAP smoke model. Volcanic emissions: NOAA/CIMSS Volcanic Cloud Monitoring, Washington VAAC, OMI SO2, NASA Global Sulfur Dioxide Monitoring, SACS (BIRA-IASB/ESA). NUCAPS temperature/moisture soundings; tropical cyclone products (CIRA TC Real-time, SSEC/CIMSS Tropic, eTRAP).

**GEOSTATIONARY OPERATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL SATELLITES—R SERIES** (link, goes-r.gov, NOAA/NASA): the GOES-R four-satellite program (GOES-R/S/T/U, becoming GOES-16/17/18/19) extending GOES service through 2036. Instruments: Advanced Baseline Imager (ABI), Extreme Ultraviolet and X-ray Irradiance Sensors (EXIS), Geostationary Lightning Mapper (GLM), Magnetometer (MAG), Space Environment In-Situ Suite (SEISS), Solar Ultraviolet Imager (SUVI). Data relay services: Data Collection System (DCS), GOES Rebroadcast (GRB, replacing GVAR), HRIT/EMWIN, and SARSAT search-and-rescue. Covers visible/infrared/water-vapor channel physics, geostationary (35,800 km) vs polar orbits, GOES East at 75.2°W and GOES West at 137.0°W, launch history (GOES-R Nov 19 2016; GOES-S Mar 1 2018; GOES-T Mar 1 2022 on Atlas V 541; GOES-U Jun 25 2024 on Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center), GOES-19 becoming operational GOES East on April 7 2025, GOES-16 as on-orbit backup, and news items: LightningCast AI lightning prediction, Next Generation Fire System (NGFS) AI wildfire detection, CCOR-1 compact coronagraph for coronal mass ejections/geomagnetic storms, GeoXO next-generation program, hurricanes Katrina/Erin/Patricia/Helene/Milton coverage, Canadian wildfire smoke and pyrocumulonimbus, atmospheric rivers, 2025 Atlantic hurricane season, 50 years of GOES, AMS SatMOC "Satellites and Fire" short course, El Niño SciJinks video.

**Lecture Materials (notebook)**: `Lecture1_Weather_Hazards_Monitoring.ipynb` from the GitHub repo MAbdelkader94/Monitoring-Weather-Hazards-with-Geostationary-Satellites — "Weather Hazards Monitoring with GOES-R Data." A fill-in-the-blank exercise notebook using the **SatPy** library for reading, manipulating, and visualizing weather-satellite data. Code downloads GOES-16 ABI-L1b-RadF NetCDF files (channels C01–C16, OR_ABI-L1b-RadF-M6 filenames) from the noaa-goes16 Amazon S3 bucket using `requests` and `os`; searches directories with `glob`; suppresses `warnings`; and uses SatPy's `Scene` object and `find_files_and_readers` with the `abi_l1b` reader (Level 1b) and `abi_l2_nc` reader (Level 2 ABI-L2-CMIPF NetCDF data). Emphasizes that SatPy requires original filenames. Exercises cover loading channels C01–C16 via list comprehension, `available_composite_names`, loading and showing the `airmass` composite, inspecting DataID keys (name, WavelengthRange, resolution, calibration), area definitions for C13 (infrared), C01 (~0.47 µm visible), and C02 (~0.64 µm visible), loading `natural_color`, resampling (`scn.resample`) to the C13 grid, cropping with `ll_bbox` bounding boxes, and visualizing hazard composites: `ash` (volcanic ash), `so2` (sulfur dioxide), `cira_fire_temperature` (wildfire thermal detection), and `land_cloud_fire`. Also uses `%matplotlib inline`, `matplotlib.pyplot`, and `datetime`.

Covers Scene object creation, file discovery, composites (natural color, airmass, ash, so2, fire temperature, land-cloud-fire), resampling, cropping, and hazard visualization techniques.
