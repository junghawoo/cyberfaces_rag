Climate-efficient 
Agriculture R&D
Diego S. Cardoso

Motivation
• AEPP paper (Fuglie et al., 2022): Multiple scenarios of Ag R&D 
investment growth
• Outcomes: reduced emissions and undernutrition
• In particular, reduced cost of climate policy
• But cost reductions are paid for by R&D expenditures
• This exercise: Do climate policy cost reductions via Ag R&D pay off?

Method
• Scenario S0 is the same as in AEPP: R&D trends are business-as-usual (BAU)
• 4 additional scenarios
• S1 to S4: R&D investments grow 1 to 4% annually
• Outcomes of interest:
• Average abatement cost: $ spent in R&D / GHG emission
• Marginal abatement cost: additional $ spent in R&D / additional GHG 
emission
• Two metrics:
• Accounting costs: just dollar value of R&D expenditure
• Total welfare costs: R&D expenditures + changes in private surplus

Results: aggregate reductions

Results: abatement costs

“Conclusion”
• R&D has opposing effects on private surplus: Q,  P 
• Consumers are mostly happy (higher surplus)
• Producers are mostly unhappy, especially land-owners
• The value of land decreases sharply
• Here, producer losses > consumer gains
• But this is a slightly more elaborated ‘back-of-the-envelope’ exercise
• I had to rely on quick fixes to derive surplus expressions
• Need to modify model assumptions to do proper welfare analysis
• In any case, there will be tension between land value loss and 
consumer surplus
• What else to consider?

Outline
1. Our story
2. How do we simulate water restrictions and R&D
3. The results
4. Discussion on the timing of shocks

Our story
• Changes in population growth and income has an impact on food consumption 
and production. 
• Increasing the demand for agricultural products                 increases the demand 
of inputs (water resources).
• In the USA, most regions using ground water for irrigation purposes, use it at an 
unsustainable level.
• Our experiment focuses on reducing the unsustainable use of ground water for 
irrigation purposes in the USA and thus improve regions ability to meet future 
water needs.

How to model our story?
Our experiment has two parts:
• First, we create a world in 2050, given changes in:
•
Regional population growth
•
Regional income growth
•
Productivity improvements in livestock and processed foods
•
Productivity improvement in the production of crops (without R&D)
•
Introduce climate change
•
Secondly, we address impact on ground water use.
•
How do we improve ground water storage?
•
Introducing water restrictions on unsustainable ground water use                              
for irrigated crop production.
•
Address, through R&D, the negative impact on  irrigated crop                     
production.
