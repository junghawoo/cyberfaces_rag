Public Domain Dedication and License (PDDL): 
●
Allows users to freely use, modify, and distribute data without any legal restrictions or obligations. 
●
Similar to the public domain license.
Attribution License (ODC-By): 
●
Allows users to freely use, modify, and distribute data, provided that they attribute the original creator. 
●
This license is recommended for data that the creator wishes to be freely shared, but still wants to be 
credited for their work.
Open Database License (ODbL): 
●
Specifically designed for databases and regulates the use and sharing of data within the database. 
●
Requires users to attribute the database creator and share any modifications to the database under the 
same terms. 
●
It is recommended for databases that contain valuable data that the creator wishes to share but also wants 
to protect.

MIT license 
●
The MIT License is a popular open-source license that allows users to freely use, modify, and
distribute software or other creative works.
●
Permissive License: The MIT License is a permissive license that allows anyone to use the
licensed software or work for any purpose, including commercial use, without restriction.
●
Modification: The MIT License allows users to modify the licensed software or work and
distribute the modified version under any license they choose, including proprietary licenses.
However, the modified version must include the original copyright notice and license terms.
●
Redistribution: The MIT License allows users to redistribute the licensed software or work,
either in its original or modified form, without any additional restrictions or requirements.
●
Compatibility: The MIT License is compatible with many other open-source licenses, allowing
users to combine and distribute software or works under multiple licenses.
23

Comparison 
Use
Creative Commons, Open Data Commons, and MIT licenses are open-source licenses that 
allow users to access and use software or creative works without restrictions. Proprietary 
licenses, on the other hand, restrict the use of software or creative works to the license holder, 
and EULAs restrict the use of software or content to the end user.
Modification
Creative Commons, Open Data Commons, and MIT licenses allow users to modify software 
or creative works and distribute them under different licenses. Proprietary licenses usually 
restrict the modification of software or creative works.
Attribution
Creative Commons licenses require users to attribute the original author of the work when 
using or distributing it. Open Data Commons licenses may or may not require attribution, 
depending on the license used. Proprietary licenses may or may not require attribution, 
depending on the terms of the license. MIT licenses require that the original copyright notice 
and license terms are included in any modifications or redistributions.
Compatibility
Creative Commons, Open Data Commons, and MIT licenses are compatible with many other 
open-source licenses. Proprietary licenses are usually not compatible with open-source 
licenses.
Scope
Creative Commons and Open Data Commons licenses are mainly used for works such as 
text, images, and data. MIT License is mainly used for software. Proprietary licenses and 
EULAs are used for both software and creative works.
