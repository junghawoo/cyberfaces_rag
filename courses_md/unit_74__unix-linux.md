# Unix/Linux

## Text content

### What is Unix/Linux?
Unix is a computer operating system. At the time of its creation, it was most known for its portability, its ability to multitask and for its ability to allow multiple users. Unix was developed in the late 1960's by Bell labs, and many of its aspects continue to live on in many of our devices today. One such aspect is the "Unix Philosophy," the idea that there are multiple small modules that each perform one specific task very well. By combining these small modules, Unix can handle more complex tasks. Today, it would be hard to find an original version of Unix. Unix can be found incorporated into many operating systems such as macOS, Solaris and AIX. Many of these descendants of Unix are closed source, but some, like Linux, are open source. Linux is open sourced, free, and most closely related to Unix. There are not many differences between Unix and Linux. Linux was written from scratch so that it acts like Unix, but does not have any Unix code in it.
