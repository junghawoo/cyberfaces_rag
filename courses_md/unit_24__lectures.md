### Slide 2
Motivation
Despite abundant and affordable food throughout the developed world, currently 12.9 percent of the population in developing countries is undernourished (World Food Program 2016)
By 2050, world population is expected to increase by 33 percent, from 7.3 to 9.7 billion (United Nations 2015)
When coupled with increases in income and changing diets, this may translate into 60-100 percent growth in the demand for agricultural products (Alexandratos and Bruinsma 2012, Tilman et al. 2011)
Evolution in agricultural productivity growth will play critical role in meeting this demand

### Slide 3
Motivation
Since the 1950s, increased agricultural productivity has allowed food availability to outpace demand on a global scale, resulting in a long run downward trend in world prices
Public and private investments into agricultural research and development (R&D) have been the foundation for this achievement
High return on the investments is taken as evidence of underinvestment and suggests that increasing investment will further increase agricultural output
Long lag in response of agricultural productivity to investment in R&D (Alston et al. 2010, Baldos et al. 2015); long run planning is required
The response depends on uncertain climate change
Drivers of future food demand are very uncertain as shown by SSPs:
Spread between low and high global population levels in 2100 may be as high as 5.8 billion people 
Average global per capita income in 2100 ranges from 22 and 138 thousand 2005USD

### Slide 5
Methodology (overview)
Parametrize lagged relationship between R&D spending and agricultural productivity, represented by total factor productivity (TFP),  using historical data 
Develop dynamic global model of land use (motivated by Steinbuks and Hertel 2013)
Build into the model TFP production function (the calibrated relationship between R&D spending and agricultural TFP) 
In the model both TFP and R&D expenditures are endogenous variables
Represent uncertainty in future drivers of food demand and supply 
Find optimal path of R&D and TFP in 21st century

### Slide 14
Discussion
In this analysis, uncertainty in climate change plays a relatively small role
Population in 2100 varies by factor of 2, while for change in global surface temperature difference between most extreme SSPs is 1.5 degree Celsius
SSPs temperature range does not reflect the full range of possible temperature outcomes in 2100 
Uncertainty in the response of agricultural yields to higher temperatures is not factored into our analysis
Uncertainty in the demand response to higher incomes, as well as in the response of productivity to R&D—both factors which, if added to the analysis, would increase the uncertainty associated with demographic and economic growth
Global scale of the analysis
In reality, lagged relationship between R&D and TFP, population and income growth, and climate impacts are region specific

### Slide 7
Problem: Enhancing Global Production on a ~Fixed Land Base & the Decline in Relative Rate of Yield Increase per Area
As farmers’ yields move up towards the yield potential threshold, it becomes more difficult to sustain further yield gain because it requires fine tuning of many different facets of mgmt/production (e.g., the “low hanging fruit” like N fertilizer have been “picked”)
Moving high yield even higher is often difficult to achieve in farmer’s fields-the associated marginal costs, labor requirements, risks and environmental impacts may outweigh the benefits.
Meaningful increases in grain production will require significant increases in average yields in countries with large current yield gaps.
Paradoxically, many countries exhibiting the largest gaps have  poor access to technology, infrastructure and capital

### Slide 10
A Few Definitions…..
Yield Potential (Yp): yield of a cultivar when nutrients, water,.. are non-limiting and pests are controlled.
Water-limited yield potential (Yw): Same as Yp, but rainfed so water stress may impact yield. Influenced by soil type, landscape position in field, precipitation,…
Average Yield (Ya): Yield actually achieved on a farmers field.
Yield Gap: Yg=Yp (irrigated) or Yw (rainfed) minus Ya.

### Slide 11
Yield potential (Yp) of irrigated crops is determined by primarily by solar radiation, temperature, & growing season. Under rainfed conditions, the water-limited yield (Yw) represents the ceiling yield. Average yields (Ya) result from pests, nutrients, and water limiting yield.
