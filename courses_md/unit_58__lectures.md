SIMPLE’s origin: analytical model developed for AAEA presidential address –
provided basic economic insights
SIMPLE framework (development led by Uris Baldos) based on idea of avoiding 
unnecessary complexity; When a new issue arises, generate a new variant of 
SIMPLE instead of adding on to existing model
First application aimed at understanding why global models were projecting so 
much land use change:
- Validate the model backwards in time: 2006-1961
- Then use historical laboratory to understand consequences of restrictions imposed in 
leading models at the time:
Absence of price sensitivity of demand 
Absence of endogenous intensification 
- These omissions led to over-prediction of historical land conversion
Validation against regional outcomes led to focus on trade and segmentation of 
markets (to be discussed this morning)

Core model:
-

Motivation
There are other models that offer multi-scale analysis of land & water 
sustainability challenges. SIMPLE-G does not replace them as they 
are generally much larger and more complex.
SIMPLE-G strengths and value-added come through:
Flexibility: Framework can be readily adapted to problem at hand
Analytical tractability: Even the more complex versions of SIMPLE-G 
can be numerically collapsed to a version featuring intensive and 
extensive margins of supply, with possible shifts in these curves. This 
maps nicely to many of the sustainability challenges we face today.
Analytical tractability is key to:
Allowing us to dig into particular grid cell outcomes and explain 
differences across grid cells
Understanding the role of key parameters in governing outcomes
When paired with visualization and the ‘mini-model’ this offers a 
powerful set of tools for satisfying analysis of local outcomes

A Key Role for Factor Markets
Broadly group inputs into:

Those tied to a given location: e.g., land and water

And those that are potentially mobile: e.g., labor and capital
Location-specific inputs tend to be more inelastically supplied

As grid cell output expands, land rents are quickly bid up

Conservation policies also tend to target these inputs (land and water)
Other inputs (often termed variable inputs) are more price-elastic

Contraction in output is reflected in relatively greater quantity adjustment, 
less change in factor price
For starters, we will develop analytics assuming that the non-Land inputs are 
in perfectly elastic supply at the grid level

All the of the adjustment comes on the quantity side, with prices exogenous 
to the grid cell/farm

Then show how this changes in a more realistic setting where some inputs 
(e.g., labor) may be location-specific

The critical role of nonland input markets
The assumption that nonland input prices are invariant 
to local developments is unrealistic
Some prices are nationally determined (e.g., petroleum)
But many depend on local conditions (e.g., labor)
This is critically important for:
A farm’s ability to respond to higher prices/new 
opportunities
The effectiveness of a conservation policy
Local economies and the distributional impacts of policies
It complicates the analytics, but the intuition is similar
Consider the impact of two different shocks to grid cell:
Output price shock
Conservation policy shock

when non-land prices adjust
Q: What does it mean non-land prices ‘adjust’?
A: When we assume non-land prices are constant, we assume non-land inputs freely 
adjust to any price shocks. A higher output price implies greater demand for both 
inputs i.e. more labor is available for hire at the prevailing wage. This maybe true in 
case of prices of some inputs (say fertilizer) but not necessarily labor due to potential 
labor market rigidities!
When we allow non-land price to ‘adjust’, we allow for some non-land factor market 
rigidities.

Large models and computation speed
Speed of solution is a challenging issue for large models with 
millions of grid cells.
For a large model with complex interconnections between the 
unknown variables, the issue is more critical (e.g. market response 
to farmers decision and farmers response to market equilibrium)
People may design different algorithms to reduce the solution 
time. There are also techniques for better use of computational 
resources (CPU, GPU, and memory)
We use a method called condensation…

What is condensation
Reducing the model size before solving the linearized system of 
equations
It involves
Omit
Substitute
Backsolve



How effective is condensation?
For the SIMPLE-G (with complementary slackness), with 4 million 
unknowns for ~75,000 grid cells.
45 minutes without condensation
2 minutes with condensation
For a simplified model, with 6 million unknown and 1,000,000 grid cells
10 minutes without condensation
0.5 minute with condensation
*On a laptop with 8th gen core i7 and 32G memory

How GEMPACK/TABLO works
You introduce variables and equations
In either levels or percentage changes (e.g. PCROP, p_PCROP)
Stage 1: Check
TABLO checks your input file and reporting any error it finds
It automatically linearizes all the level equations 
Stage 2: Condensation
TABLO substitute variables in the order you specify
Stage 3: Code generation

How we do it: substitution
If we re-write the system: C.z = 0
We try to reduce the size (number of rows and/or columns) of C matrix.
1.
substitute out variables
Those that are to be endogenous
2.
omit variables: 
Those that are to be exogenous and not shocked in a group of simulations

Example
Suppose you want to substitute out (linear) variable x (all components of it) using the 
(linearized) equation.
x(i) = A(i)*y(i) + z(i)
TABLO will replace every occurrence of a component of x in the other (linearized) 
EQUATIONs For example, the equation:
B(i)*(x(i) + y(i)) = 0
Becomes:
B(i)*([A(i)*y(i)+z(i)] + y(i)) = 0

Omit, Backsolve or Substitution? 
Omit:
If all components of a (linear) variable x(i) are to be exogenous and not shocked, all values 
(changes or percentage changes) in the linearized equations will be zero.
Substitute:
If you are not interested in reporting the value of the variable
Backsolve:
You want to see the value of the variable which was substituted out
If you plan to use AnalyseGE
It is a post simulation process and takes some resources to report. 
It is best to mark for backsolving only variables you think you will need to examine or report.

What is an emulator?
“Emulation is the process of mimicking the outwardly observable 
behavior to match an existing target. The internal state of the emulation 
mechanism does not have to accurately reflect the internal state of the 
target which it is emulating.
Simulation, on the other hand, involves modeling the underlying state 
of the target. The end result of a good simulation is that the simulation 
model will emulate the target which it is simulating.”
