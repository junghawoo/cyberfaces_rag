# HANDS-ON LAB assignment

Step-by-step hands-on lab module from the SIMPLE-G Short Course (Purdue University, September 2019), containing three lab documents on running and analyzing SIMPLE/SIMPLE-G economic models.

**Lab 1 — Replicate and discuss GEC paper** (source file: `GEC Lab Assignment - African Green Revolution.doc`), "Food and Environmental Security in a Globalizing Economy," prepared by Thomas Hertel and Uris Baldos. Compares the segmented markets vs. integrated markets versions of the SIMPLE model, contrasting the frictionless "bathtub"/law-of-one-price world trade model with segmented agricultural markets shaped by tariffs, consumer subsidies, and market access. Core references: Hertel & Baldos (2016) "Attaining Food and Environmental Security in an Era of Globalization," Global Environmental Change 41:195-205, and Hertel, Ramankutty & Baldos (2014) PNAS 111(38) on how global market integration affects an African Green Revolution's cropland and CO2 emissions impacts. Introduces parameters ESUBr (substitution elasticity between local and global crops) and ETRANSr (transformation elasticity) read from file LANDPARM, GEMPACK/TABLO equations (E_QDCROPr, E_QCONS_CRP_GLB, E_QSCROP_LOC, E_QSCROP_GLB) and variables PCROP_loc, PCROPW, QSCROPr, QDCROPr. Includes a price transmission elasticity table for 15 regions (e.g., Sub-Saharan Africa 0.189, EU 0.635, Japan+Korea 0.636, China 0.165). Students run 2006-2050 baseline projections using databases LANDDATA-2006-SEG.HAR / LANDDATA-2006-INT.HAR, closure Projections.cls, shocks "Baseline 2006-2050.shf", solving with Gragg 2-4-6 (2 subintervals, automatic accuracy), viewing results in VIEWHAR; then run African Green Revolution experiments (LANDDATA-2050-INT.har, 50_GR_SSA.cls, 50_GR_SSA.shk) shocking SSA crop productivity. Tables and discussion questions cover crop price, nonfarm undernutrition, cropland, and terrestrial carbon emissions globally and for SSA/China under segmented vs. integrated markets.

**Lab 2 — Understanding the SIMPLE-G-US-CS at the level of individual grid cells** (source file: `Hands-On Lab with CS model-final.docx`), by Thomas Hertel and Jing Liu. Uses a 10-grid-cell "mini" SIMPLE-G-US-CS (corn-soy) model vs. the full gridded model on GeoHub. References: Baldos, Haqiqi, Hertel, Horridge & Liu (2019) "SIMPLE-G: A Multiscale Framework..." (Environmental Modelling & Software, in prep) and Liu et al. (2018) PPRI Policy Brief on managing nitrogen losses from corn production. Covers the nested production tree (nitrogen fertilizer vs. augmented land; intensive vs. extensive margins), equations E_QNITROgl, E_PLANDgl, E_PWATERgl, E_PNLANDgl, E_QCROPgl, zero-profit conditions, and decompositions E_p_QCROP_A and E_p_QNITROgl_A. Experiments: +10% US corn/soy price shock (closure simple-mini.cls, Gragg 2-4-6, analysis in AnalyseGE), a $100/ton nitrate leaching tax (NTAX shock, leaching intensity LEACHINTgl), and wetland restoration/land set-aside conservation policy. Tables report land supply elasticity, land cost share, substitution elasticity, PAUGLAND, QAUGLAND, QNITRO, QCROP for irrigated and rainfed practices across Corn Belt grid cells KS, SD, NE, MN, IA, MO, WI, IL, IN, OH.

**Lab 3 — Learning about Water in SIMPLE-G-US at the level of individual grid cells** (source file: `Hands-On Lab with mini Water model-final.docx`), by Iman Haqiqi. Introduces the water module of SIMPLE-G (Simplified International Model of agricultural Prices, Land use and the Environment). Application reference: Haqiqi, Bowling, Jame, Hertel, Baldos & Liu (2018) "Global Drivers of Land and Water Sustainability Stresses at Mid-Century," Purdue Policy Research Institute Policy Brief. Requires SIMPLE-G-US-mini.zip, RunGEM from GEMPACK (copsmodels.com), TABmate, and a spreadsheet (e.g., Google Sheets). Covers the nested water production structure (QWATERgl ~ QWWDRLgl water withdrawal + QWEQPTgl irrigation equipment; QLANDWTRgl; QAUGLANDgl), the AgWater-Identity decomposing water use into irrigated area, yield, and water-per-crop (WATperCROPgl, YIELDgl), and land supply/allocation equations. Exercises: (1) inspect GRIDPARM.HAR parameters ECROPgl, EIRRIGgl, ETRANIGg, ELAND; (2) water scarcity: -10% water supply shock (watScarcity.shf); (3) global warming 3-A: -10% rainfed productivity shock p_AOCROgl (warming_rfd.shf); (4) global warming 3-B: rainfed -10%, irrigated -5%, AFWATERgl -10% (warming_all.shf). Result tables span grid cells TX, ID, CA, AR, IL, MO, ND, WA, NE, MI with cost shares (SHR_LAND, SHR_NITRO, SHR_WATER), yields, and post-shock changes in p_QCROPgl, p_QLANDgl, p_QNITROgl, p_QWWDRLgl, p_PLANDgl, p_PWATERgl. Discussion questions ask why grid cells respond heterogeneously to each shock.

## Summarized attachments

- **GEC Lab Assignment - African Green Revolution** (`GEC Lab Assignment - African Green Revolution.doc`, file): Hands-on lab assignment from the SIMPLE-G Short Course prepared by Thomas Hertel and Uris Baldos (Purdue LSCE, September 2019) on segmented vs. integrated agricultural markets. Compares the impact of an African Green Revolution shock to SSA crop productivity under two market structures, contrasting the frictionless "bathtub" model with segmented markets shaped by trade friction, tariffs, and variable regional market access. Includes USGS site examples (Cedar Creek, Yellowstone River), 15-region price transmission elasticity table, GEMPACK/TABLO equations (E_QDCROPr, E_QCONS_CRP_GLB, E_QSCROP_LOC, E_QSCROP_GLB), parameters ESUBr and ETRANSr, and baseline/counterfactual simulation instructions with VIEWHAR output viewing and structured discussion questions on crop price, undernutrition, cropland, and terrestrial carbon emissions.

- **Hands-On Lab with CS model-final.docx** (`Hands-On Lab with CS model-final.docx`, file): Detailed lab manual by Thomas Hertel and Jing Liu introducing the 10-grid-cell "mini" SIMPLE-G-US-CS (corn-soy) model for understanding production at the individual grid cell level. References Baldos, Haqiqi, Hertel, Horridge & Liu (2019) "SIMPLE-G: A Multiscale Framework..." (Environmental Modelling & Software, in prep). Covers the nested production tree (nitrogen vs. augmented land), intensive vs. extensive margin decompositions, equations E_QNITROgl, E_PLANDgl, E_PWATERgl, E_PNLANDgl, E_QCROPgl, and zero-profit conditions. Experiments: +10% US corn/soy price shock with closure simple-mini.cls and Gragg 2-4-6 solution method; analysis in AnalyseGE; a $100/ton nitrate leaching tax (NTAX) with variable leaching intensity LEACHINTgl across Corn Belt grid cells (KS, SD, NE, MN, IA, MO, WI, IL, IN, OH); and wetland restoration conservation policy with 1% land set-aside. Tables report land supply elasticity, land cost share, substitution elasticity, and results for PAUGLAND, QAUGLAND, QNITRO, QCROP across irrigated and rainfed practices.

- **Hands-On Lab with mini Water model-final.docx** (`Hands-On Lab with mini Water model-final.docx`, file): Lab manual by Iman Haqiqi (Purdue LSCE, September 2019) introducing the water module of SIMPLE-G. References Haqiqi, Bowling, Jame, Hertel, Baldos & Liu (2018) "Global Drivers of Land and Water Sustainability Stresses at Mid-Century," PPRI Policy Brief 4(1). Requires SIMPLE-G-US-mini.zip, RunGEM, TABmate, and spreadsheet software. Covers the water production nested structure (QWATERgl components, QLANDWTRgl, QAUGLANDgl), the AgWater-Identity decomposition (water withdrawal = irrigated area × yield × water-per-crop), and four exercises: (1) GRIDPARM.HAR parameter inspection (ECROPgl, EIRRIGgl, ETRANIGg, ELAND); (2) water scarcity shock (-10% supply); (3) global warming 3-A (-10% rainfed productivity); (4) global warming 3-B (rainfed -10%, irrigated -5%, water requirement +10%). Post-shock result tables include cost shares (SHR_LAND, SHR_NITRO, SHR_WATER), yields, water use components, and responses across nine US grid cells (TX, ID, CA, AR, IL, MO, ND, WA, NE, MI).
