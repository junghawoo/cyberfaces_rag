# Climate Change Impacts in Agriculture

This unit covers climate change impacts on crop production, economic modeling, and adaptation strategies based on Springer research and GEMPACK modeling software.

## Climate Change Impacts in Agriculture (Book Chapter)

**Chapter Reference**: Hertel, T.W., Baldos, U.L.C. (2016). Climate Change Impacts in Agriculture. In: Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet. Springer, Cham. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_6. Available at: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_6. ISBN: 978-3-319-22661-3 (Print), 978-3-319-22662-0 (Online). Pages 69-84. Publishers: Springer International Publishing Switzerland (Copyright 2016). Supplemental learning resources: https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change.

**Authors**: Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos (Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN).

**Content Overview**: Reviews evidence on climate change consequences for temperature and precipitation; examines mechanisms affecting crop production: temperature effects on crop development, elevated CO2 effects on photosynthesis/respiration, water stress from higher temperatures/uneven rainfall, extreme temperature damage, pest/disease crop damage. Quantifies impacts historically; uses SIMPLE model for global climate change simulations. Analyzes land use and food price impacts; explores adaptation responses including irrigation investment and heat/drought-tolerant crop variety development via agricultural research funding. Shows successful adaptation offers mitigation benefits: improved yields reduce land conversion need, lowering land-use-change emissions.

**Key Concepts**: Climate change impacts on crops, CO2 fertilization and crop productivity, agricultural adaptation to climate change, climate change and land use, research and development responses.

**Keywords**: Climate impacts on crops, CO2 fertilization effects, adaptation strategies, land-use linkages, R&D investment, crop modeling.

**Referenced Methodologies and Key Authors** (extensive bibliography includes):
- Crop production models: DSSAT (Jones et al. 2003), AgMIP (Rosenzweig et al. 2013)
- Climate analysis: IPCC (2014), Alexander et al. 2006 (daily extremes), Held & Soden 2006 (hydrological cycle)
- Temperature-yield relationships: Schlenker & Roberts 2009 (PNAS nonlinear temperature effects), Lobell et al. 2013 (extreme heat maize), Luo 2011 (temperature thresholds)
- Yield-drought sensitivity: Lobell et al. 2014 (Science greater drought sensitivity with yield increase)
- CO2 effects: Leakey 2009 (C4 crops), Long 1991 (photosynthesis responses), Matsui et al. 1997 (rice spikelet sterility), Rosenthal et al. 2012 (cassava FACE)
- Agricultural economics: Mendelsohn et al. 1994 (Ricardian analysis), Schlenker et al. 2005 (irrigation hedonic approach), Antle & Capalbo 2010 (policy perspective), Hertel & Lobell 2014 (adaptation modeling practice)
- Regional analysis: Ahmed et al. 2012 (Tanzania), Burgess & Donaldson 2010 (India famine era), Thornton et al. 2011 (SSA 4°C world), Washington et al. 2006 (African climate change)
- Agricultural adaptation: Olmstead & Rhode 2011 (wheat 1839-2009), Ortiz et al. 2008 (wheat heat tolerance), Kosina et al. 2007 (stakeholder perception), Cooper et al. 2008 (SSA rainfed farming)
- Research investment: Pardey 2006 (agricultural R&D divide), Alston et al. 2010 & 2008 (research lags spillovers), Thirtle et al. 2003 (productivity-poverty reduction), Nelson & IFPRI 2009 (adaptation costs), Nelson et al. 2010 (2050 scenarios)
- Pest/disease: Hannukkala et al. 2007 (late blight potato Finland), Ziska et al. 2011 (invasive species)
- Trade/policy: Anderson & Nelgen 2011 (food price stabilization), Ivanic & Martin 2008 (food prices poverty), World Bank 2010 (development climate change)

**Supporting Resources**: https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change (supplemental learning materials).

## GEMPACK Software

**Purpose**: Computational General Equilibrium (CGE) modeling software for economic analysis and policy simulation (https://www.copsmodels.com/gplapsoft.htm).

**Pre-Course Installation Requirements**: Training course participants must install GEMPACK on laptops prior to course start for multiple reasons: surface software problems early during course sessions (not during busy instruction), address issues before course start, handle administrator access requirements (potentially needing IT department assistance), and resolve corporate IT policy conflicts preemptively.

**Version Requirements**: Course requires GEMPACK Release 11.0 or later. Check version via menu Help...GEMPACK licence (TABmate or ViewHAR). Release 10 or earlier requires upgrading to Release 11; instructions provided for renaming existing folders for backward compatibility.

**Installation Steps**: Download and read quickinst.pdf (simplified training-course-specific instructions). Download Trial Edition Limited Executable-Image Version from COPSM Models website. Follow QuickInst instructions (shorter than full GPInstall.pdf). Complete Section 3 of QuickInst: verify working installation by running model simulation (critical verification step).

**Trial Edition Features**: Includes all main GEMPACK programs for full modeling range; model size limitation not problematic during course. Temporary license 6 months from download date (sufficient for course completion); often participants receive longer-lasting license during course.

**Additional Software**: Some courses require supplementary programs: RunDynam (dynamic simulations), RunGTAP (GTAP model solving), or minimal free training software (Practical GE Melbourne course). Install GEMPACK first; other programs may require pre-existing GEMPACK.

**Course Materials**: Participants provided various files for course exercises at course start. Course CD/USB key supplied during most courses containing: required software, exercise files, supplementary material (primarily for post-course use). Pre-course software installation required; supplementary materials mainly referenced after course.

**License Upgrading**: Trial edition temporary license 6 months from download. Course participants often entitled to better license: course-specific license 12 months from course start (provided beforehand via email or upon arrival).

**System Requirements**: Specific laptop requirements documented on COPSM website. Most Windows laptops adequate. May require administrator access rights for installation (potentially unavailable for organization-configured laptops; IT assistance needed).

**Related Resources**: Training course page, Laptop requirements documentation, Executable-Image Version information, Practical GE course materials.

**Keywords**: CGE modeling, GEMPACK software, computational economics, policy simulation, general equilibrium analysis, course preparation, model development.

## Summarized attachments
- **Book chapter: Climate Change Impacts in Agriculture** (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_6, URL): Springer book chapter authored by Hertel and Baldos examining climate change effects on crop production through temperature changes, CO2 impacts on photosynthesis, water stress, and pest damage, with historical quantification and global SIMPLE model simulations showing adaptation strategies including irrigation investment and crop breeding for heat and drought tolerance.
- **GEMPACK Software** (https://www.copsmodels.com/gplapsoft.htm, software tool): Computational General Equilibrium modeling software for economic analysis and policy simulation, requiring pre-course installation with version 11.0 or later, trial edition includes main programs with 6-month temporary license suitable for training course completion.
