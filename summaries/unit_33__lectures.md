# Lectures

**Description:** Instructor presentations for the SIMPLE-G Short Course (Purdue University, West Lafayette, September 16-20, 2019). This module bundles ten lecture files (PDF and PPTX) covering the theory, analytics, production structure, parameterization, data workflow, and policy applications of the SIMPLE and SIMPLE-G economic models.

**Hertel: "Analytics of the Gridded Model.pdf"** (Analytical Foundations of SIMPLE-G, Thomas Hertel). Motivates SIMPLE-G as inspired by climate science (GCMs providing boundary conditions to high-resolution RCMs; SIMPLE = coarse GCM analog nested within a gridded model solved simultaneously). Derives analytical results for grid-cell responses to crop price changes and a tax on excess nitrogen use, covering land returns, output changes, intensive vs. extensive margins of supply, fertilizer (N) leaching rates, and de-intensification. Provides a notational mapping to GEMPACK/TABLO variables: p_QCROPgl, p_QAUGLANDgl, p_QNITROgl, p_PCROPr(GRID2REG(g)), p_PAUGLANDgl, p_PNITROgl, ECROPgl, ELANDg, SHRAUGLANDgl, and equation E_p_QCROP_A using SHR_ALANDgl and EALANDgl.

**Haqiqi: "Condensation_2019-09-15.pdf"** (Iman Haqiqi). Explains condensation for solving large GEMPACK/TABLO models with millions of unknowns across ~75,000 to 1,000,000 grid cells; condensation (omit, substitute, backsolve) cut solution time from 45 min to 2 min (and 10 min to 0.5 min) on a laptop with 8th-gen core i7 and 32GB memory. Describes TABLO stages (Check/linearize, Condensation, Code generation), the C.z=0 system reduction, backsolving for AnalyseGE, condensation info file status (E_QCONS, E_QNITROgl, E_QLANDgl BACKSOLVING; E_PNITROgl ELIMINATED), and limitations on order of substitution.

**Baldos: "Economic Framework Recap 09-04-2019.pdf"** (Uris Baldos). Recaps the SIMPLE theoretical framework: agriculture as a single sector using land and non-land inputs; three key drivers (demand growth, derived demand/trend yield growth, land supply shifts) and three economic responses (price elasticity of food demand, extensive margin/land supply elasticity, intensive margin/elasticity of substitution). References Hertel (2011) "Global Supply and Demand for Land in 2050: A Perfect Storm?" AAEA Presidential Address. Covers crop demand equations (E_QPC per capita commodity demand p_QPC = EOP*p_P + EINC*p_INC_PC; regional demand p_QCONS; supply-demand balance), econometric food demand elasticities from Mohammed et al. (2011) (range 0.86-0.30 across 144 countries, global avg 0.53 in 2005 using ICP data), extensive margin (ELAND, E_PLANDg), intensive margin (isoquant/isocost, expansion vs. substitution effects, endogenous yield growth).

**Liu: "Introduce gridded model theory and gridded indexing.pdf"** (Jing Liu). Introduces SIMPLE-G (Simplified International Model of agricultural Prices, Land use and the Environment): demand side (per capita demand, population, income INC, biofuels, food security/prices) and supply side (nitrogen, land-water, input substitution/prices, productivity shocks from Temp/Precip/CO2, Ag R&D). Presents the SIMPLE-G mini model of 10 grid cells from top-10 corn states (KS, SD, NE, MN, IA, MO, WI, IL, IN, OH) with per-grid parameters (elasticity of land supply, elasticity of substitution between N and augmented-land, cost share of N). CES cost-minimization; equations E_QNITROgl, E_QAUGLANDgl, E_QNITROr, E_PAUGLANDgl, E_QCROPgl (zero-profit). Uses 15 SIMPLE regions, disaggregates continental US to 5 arcmin; GRID2REG mapping read from GRIDSETS header "MAP1"; leaching cost formula E_PNITROgl using LEACHINTgl and NLTAXr.

**Baldos: "Market Segmentation 09-04-2019 .pptx"** (31 slides, Uris Baldos). Market segmentation in SIMPLE: integrated vs. segmented markets, trade costs, tariffs/export taxes, ESUB=3 substitution parameter. Uses GTAP and FAO data circa 2006; historical validation 1961-2006 using FAOSTAT (2014) and World Bank GEM Database (2014), referencing Hertel and Baldos (2016) "Attaining Food and Environmental Security in an Era of Globalization." Counterfactual analyses (North America, East Asia/China, SSA) and future projections 2006-2050 under segmentation vs. full integration.

**Haqiqi: "Parameter Estimation and Validation_2019-09-15.pdf"** (Iman Haqiqi). Three approaches to valuing water: fixed expenditure share; GTAP Water Data Base v1 (GTAP-E based, irrigated-rainfed yield gap, 5 arc min); and a new irrigated-agriculture database (statistically estimated, cost structure by water/energy/N/chemicals per land). CET rainfed-irrigated transformation elasticity for SIMPLE-G-US estimated from USDA county data clustered by water laws. Water supply elasticity via a Fréchet-type function (shape σ, asymptote α, location ε, scale κ). Validation of SIMPLE-G-W irrigated-area change vs. USDA.

**Baldos: "PB2 GLASS for NPC-08-2018.pdf"** (Uris Lantz Baldos with Thomas Hertel). Agricultural productivity growth for long-run sustainability: US farm output/productivity 2015 vs. 1965 (USDA-ERS 2017), technology life cycle of public R&D gains, ~50% of 1950-2010 output gains linked to US public R&D. Cites Baldos, Viens, Hertel, Fuglie (2018) AJAE Bayesian approach; Huffman & Evenson (2006); Heisey & Fuglie (2018); Clancy, Fuglie & Heisey Amber Waves (2016). Future TFP projections (baseline 1.2%/yr vs. 1.5%/yr with $1.1B/yr R&D increase 2020-2050) from SIMPLE-G.

**Liu: "PB3 GLASS for NPC-08-2018.pdf"** (Christopher Kucharik [U. Wisconsin-Madison], Laura Bowling, Jing Liu, with Hertel, Sadia Jame, Navin Ramankutty [UBC]). Managing nitrate leaching in the Corn Belt: nitrate transport via Mississippi River Basin to Gulf of Mexico dead zones (hypoxia); US EPA Hypoxia Task Force 45% N-load reduction target by 2035. Uses Agro-IBIS biophysical model joined to SIMPLE-G-US economic model. Field sites DPAC, ACRE, SEPAC; Iowa Nutrient Reduction Strategy (iasoybeans.com); conservation practices (controlled drainage, bioreactor, two-stage ditch, managed wetlands, cover crops, N field trials). Analyzes leaching charge ($/lb N) scenarios (rate reduction, split N, split N + controlled drainage, split N + wetland restoration) and NUE; concludes in-field N reduction plus wetland restoration is most effective. References Booth et al. (2016) Environmental Modelling & Software 85:80-97; Alexander et al. 2008.

**Haqiqi: "Production Structure_2019-09-15.pdf"** (Iman Haqiqi). Nested CES production structure of SIMPLE-G. Builds the production tree incrementally: land + non-land (QLAND, QNLAND, ECROP); adding nitrogen (QNITRO); irrigation (QWATER); irrigation technology (QWWDRL withdrawal, QWEQPT equipment); water sources (GRDWAT groundwater, SRFWAT surface). Full tree: QCROPgl → (QAUGLANDgl, QNITROgl) via ECROPgl; QAUGLANDgl → (QNLANDgl, QLANDWTRgl) via EAUGLANDgl; QLANDWTRgl → (QLANDgl, QWATERgl) via EIRRIGgl. Equations E_QNITROgl, E_QAUGLANDgl, E_QNLANDgl, E_QLANDgl, E_PLANDWTRgl (price index with SHR_LinLWgl, SHR_WinLWgl). Technological change variables p_AOCROPr, p_AFNITROg, p_AFAUGLANDr; efficiency shock impacts (reduce demand, encourage substitution and expansion). Key assumptions: input separability and constant elasticity of substitution within nests.

**Baldos: "SIMPLE-G Data 09-04-2019.pdf"** (Uris Baldos). SIMPLE-G data workflow. Key sources: UN FAOSTAT (population, GDP, arable land, 175 crops output/harvested area), EarthStat (crop output/harvested area, year 2000, 175 crops), Global Administrative Database (GADM country boundaries), GTAP Database (cost shares, consumption/sales shares). Online workflow (data connectors, processors, web tools, online course; .har/.csv files, R, GEMPACK). Model parameters: Muhammad et al. 2011 (income/own-price elasticities, 100+ countries); Villoria and Liu 2018 (gridded US cropland supply elasticities); MIT EPPA v4 (cropland supply, other regions); Keeney & Hertel GTAP-AGR (nonland supply). Model shocks: population (World Population Prospects 2017 Revision, SSP scenarios, KC & Lutz), income growth, crop TFP growth (Fuglie 2018; Baldos, Fuglie, Hertel 2019), and AgMIP climate-change yield impacts (7 crop models, 5 GCMs, 5 RCPs, CO2 fertilization, 12 crops, 1971-2099, 36,000+ 0.5°x0.5° grids; AgMIP tool on mygeohub.org/tools/agmip).

**Liu: "SIMPLE-G-CS parameters.pdf"** (Jing Liu). SIMPLE-G-CS emulators and elasticities. Grid-cell parameters: cost share of N fertilizer; elasticity of substitution (N/augmented-land, water/land); price elasticities of input supply (cropland, water); nitrate leaching response (quadratic in N application). Estimation via biophysical emulators using Agro-IBIS (Kucharik 2003 Earth Interactions; THMB Terrestrial Hydrology Model with Biogeochemistry) and econometrics. Gompertz yield production function and quadratic leaching function fitted to Agro-IBIS outputs to derive σLN and σLW per grid cell. Cropland supply elasticity from spatial regression using market access (Verburg et al. 2011) as land-profitability proxy, validated against USDA and Lark et al. (ERL 2015) observed cropland expansion 2008-2012. Equation E_QNITROgl for grid-cell N demand; emphasizes heterogeneous parameters.

**Haqiqi: "Water Supply and Demand_2019-09-15.pdf"** (Iman Haqiqi). Economics of water resources. Determinants of irrigation water demand, economic water supply construction, long-run equilibrium withdrawal, interpreting "price of water," shocking water supply, spatial linkages. US irrigation extent and stable water withdrawal over 3 decades. Farmer cost minimization over N, L, W, O inputs subject to production function; water demand via nested substitution parameters (σi,y; σi,aug; σi,w). Water supply via Fréchet function (σ, α, ε, κ). Water scarcity, rainfed-yield, and combined shock diagrams. TABLO code: variables p_QWEQPTgl, p_PWEQPTgl, p_QWWDRLgl, p_PWWDRLgl, p_PWATERgl, p_QWATERgl; supply equations E_PWWDRLgl, E_PWEQPTgl (with slack variables s_QWATERg, s_QWEQPTg); demand E_QWWDRLgl, E_QWEQPTgl (ESUB_WKgl); indices E_QWATERgl, E_PWATERgl. AgWater-Identity decomposing water withdrawal into irrigated area, crop yields, water per crop; TABLO decomposition p_WAT2CROPGL_A. Data: USDA Cropland Data Layer (CDL, 30m), NASS (crop value, market value, cash rents), ERS production expenses, FRIS Farm and Ranch Irrigation Survey; USGS MODIS MIrAD-US (250m); Global Crop Water Model (GCWM). Groundwater withdrawal-to-recharge ratio and value of irrigation.
