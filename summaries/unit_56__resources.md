# Resources

This Foundation-level Resources module collects three fetched external web resources supporting a course on global change, food security, and sustainable agriculture.

**Resource 1: "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" Book chapter 4 (link)** — Springer Nature Link chapter "Economic Responses to Scarcity" (pp 41–56, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_4) by Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University (© 2016 Springer International Publishing Switzerland; Print ISBN 978-3-319-22661-3, Online ISBN 978-3-319-22662-0; Springer, Cham; Earth and Environmental Science eBook package). The abstract covers endogenous economic responses to scarcity when demand growth outpaces productivity growth: the demand margin (consumer response to higher prices), the extensive margin of supply (cropland area response to agricultural returns), and the intensive margin of supply (substituting nonland inputs for land). Simulations with the SIMPLE model show these margins interact to determine total cropland conversion, demonstrating that purely biophysical analyses overstate required cropland conversion. Draws on Hertel and Tyner (2013). Keywords: economic responses to agricultural scarcity, intensive/extensive margin of supply, food demand response to price, land-sparing technological change. The page's extensive reference list names authors and works including Ahmed/Hertel/Lubowski (GTAP Research Memorandum), Angelsen & Kaimowitz, Baldos & Hertel, Balmford, Baumert (World Resources Institute), Beckman/Keeney/Tyner, Berry & Schlenker (ICCT), Borlaug, Deininger & Byerlee (World Bank), Fabiosa (FAPRI model), Foley, FAO Statistical Database (FAOSTAT), Fulginiti & Perrin, Gibbs, Golub & Hertel (GTAP-BIO model), Goodwin, Hertel/Golub/Jones/O'Hare/Plevin/Kammen (US maize ethanol), Hertel/Ramankutty/Baldos (African Green Revolution, PNAS), Huang & Khanna, Keeney & Hertel, Lambin (Malthus vs Ricardo), Lubowski/Plantinga/Stavins, Mather & Needle (forest transition), Muhammad/Seale/Meade/Regmi (USDA ERS International Comparison Program), Naylor, Phalan (land sharing vs land sparing), Plevin (Agro-ecological zone emission factor model), Potter, Ramankutty, Rudel, Searchinger (biofuels and land-use-change emissions), Stevenson (Green Revolution land savings), Taheripour/Zhuang/Tyner/Lu, Tilman, USDA NASS Crop Statistics, Villoria & Hertel, Vitousek, West, and World Bank (Awakening Africa's Sleeping Giant; Global Economic Monitor). Supplemental learning resources are free at https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change.

**Resource 2: "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" Book chapter 5 (link)** — Springer chapter "Water, Food and Environmental Security" (pp 57–67, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5), same authors and book. Abstract: water scarcity as a key 21st-century issue; irrigated agriculture accounts for 70% of freshwater withdrawals, under 20% of cropland but about 40% of production; growing groundwater reliance and shortages; scope for improved water use efficiency; SIMPLE model simulations on irrigation restrictions and global land use change. Draws on the OECD report by Hertel and Liu (2014). Keywords: water use in agriculture, irrigation efficiency, water scarcity and food production, groundwater depletion, water and food security. References include Alexandratos & Bruinsma (FAO world agriculture towards 2030/2050), Bjornlund & McKay (water markets in Australia, Chile, US), Bruinsma, Burke & Villholth (groundwater assessment), Chao/Wu/Li (reservoir impoundment and sea level), Craig et al. (evaporation loss control, University of Southern Queensland), De Fraiture, Döll, Eshel (water/GHG burdens of meat, eggs, dairy), Fargher (Australian water markets, OECD), Füssel, Grafton, Helfer/Lemckert/Zhang, Keller & Seckler (transpiration), Liu/Hertel/Taheripour/Zhu/Ringler (international trade buffers irrigation shortfalls), Mekonnen & Hoekstra (water footprint of farm animal products), Milly, Molden (Water for Food, Water for Life; Earthscan/IWMI), National Water Commission (Australia), Pavelic, Rockström (rainfed agriculture paradigm shift), Rosegrant & Cai, Siebert (groundwater irrigation inventory), Strzepek & Boehlert, Taheripour/Hertel/Liu (irrigation and biofuel land use), Taheripour/Hurt/Tyner (livestock in transition), Taylor (groundwater and climate change), Wada (irrigation demand projections), Wurbs & Ayala (reservoir evaporation in Texas), and Zwart (global benchmark map of water productivity for wheat). Supplemental resources at https://mygeohub.org/.

**Resource 3: GLASSNET Resources (link)** — MyGeohub group page (https://mygeohub.org/groups/glassnet) for GLASSNET: "Meeting the Global Sustainable Development Goals on a Changing Planet with Limited Land and Water Resources," a network providing decision makers with data to assess actions affecting environment, economy, and local communities (SDGs). Featured researcher: Alexandra Hill, Assistant Professor of Cooperative Extension at UC Berkeley (University of California, Berkeley), who studies the US agricultural workforce and farmworker well-being (income, health, productivity, output quality, profits, workplace injuries). Featured event: GLASSNET Thematic Seminar Series, "Modelling Extremes: From Climate Change to Biophysical Impacts and Economic Implications," on extreme weather events, food security, resource management, and the global-to-local-to-global research agenda, discussing gaps in simulation models spanning climate, vegetation, crops, and socio-economic impacts and propagation through global value chains.

## Summarized attachments

- **"Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" Book chapter 4** (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_4, remote resource): Springer Nature chapter "Economic Responses to Scarcity" covering endogenous economic responses to agricultural scarcity including demand-side consumer response, extensive margin cropland area changes, and intensive margin substitution of non-land inputs for land, with SIMPLE model simulations demonstrating that biophysical analyses overstate required cropland conversion.

- **"Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" Book chapter 5** (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5, remote resource): Springer chapter "Water, Food and Environmental Security" examining water scarcity as a 21st-century sustainability issue, irrigated agriculture's 70% share of freshwater withdrawals despite covering only 20% of cropland, and groundwater overreliance with SIMPLE model simulations on irrigation constraints affecting global land use.

- **GLASSNET Resources** (https://mygeohub.org/groups/glassnet, remote resource): MyGeohub network group providing data and research on meeting UN Sustainable Development Goals on a changing planet, featuring researcher profiles and thematic seminars on modeling climate extremes and their biophysical and economic impacts on agriculture and food security.
