# Justice in Data: Machine Learning Algorithms

Bootcamp lecture module "Machine Learning Algorithms" from the Justice in Data course. Resources: slide deck "ML algorithms" (`Lecture_Friday_2_Final.pdf`), a Machine Learning Algorithms Pre-Survey link (utaedu.questionpro.com QuestionPro survey), and a Machine Learning Algorithms Recording (YouTube video trJywgw1YUo with full lecture transcript). Presenters: Steven Tanner McCullough (PhD Student Researcher), Jessica Eisma, PhD (Assistant Professor), and June Young Park, PhD (Assistant Professor), University of Texas at Arlington building-energy domain examples.

Lecture content: (1) Introduction to ML and bootcamp objectives; (2) ML algorithms — supervised learning (Decision Tree, k-Nearest Neighbors), unsupervised learning (k-means clustering), reinforcement learning (Markov Decision Process); (3) coding practice using building energy data for classification with knn and decision tree in Python.

Supervised learning maps inputs to labeled outputs (classification vs regression — transcript examples: rain prediction, pass/fail, letter grades, exam scores, temperature, streamflow, sales income). KNN (k nearest neighbor): classify by majority class of k nearest examples; earthquake vs nuclear-explosion wave classification example; overfitting with low k (complex decision boundary, outlier-sensitive) and underfitting with high k (majority-class prediction); choose k via cross-validation and confusion matrix accuracy; curse of dimensionality (kNN degrades above ~15 dimensions). Application: adaptive thermal comfort model with KNN (Xiong & Yao, Building and Environment 202, 2021, 108026; transcript adds a Raspberry Pi sensor robot collecting temperature/relative humidity and occupant comfort votes). Decision trees: root, interior, and leaf nodes; choose the most informative feature via information gain (glasses/gender/long-hair example minimizing average questions, 2.25 vs 2); application to building energy demand modeling with the RECS dataset (Yu et al., Energy and Buildings 42.10, 2010). Other supervised algorithms named: support vector machine, logistic regression (transcript explains sigmoid/logit 0-1 classification), linear regression, naive Bayes, artificial neural network.

Unsupervised learning models unlabeled data ("letting the dataset speak for itself"; Geoffrey Hinton 1996 quote). K-means clustering partitions data into k non-overlapping clusters; choosing k via the elbow method on sum of squared error (also silhouette scoring, no-free-lunch theorem). Application: load-shape profile clustering for benchmarking buildings (Park et al., Applied Energy 236, 2019, "Apples or oranges?"; k-means, bisecting k-means, GMM compared, k=3 chosen). Other algorithms: bisecting k-means, DBSCAN.

Reinforcement learning: agent-environment interaction with rewards, trial and error, Pavlov's experiment analogy; Markov Decision Process formalization with states S, actions A, rewards R, transition probabilities P (car maintenance repair/maintain/ignore example), maximizing expected future rewards. Case study: LightLearn, an RL-based occupant-centered controller for lighting (Park et al., Building and Environment, 2019) — state-action space of occupancy (Bluetooth-sensed), switch position, indoor light level (dark/comfort/bright lux thresholds), period of day; 26 episodic states; reward assignment (penalty for discomfort/energy waste, reward for comfort/energy saving); optimal policy computed nightly; tested in five offices in Austin, Texas, saving energy (82% vs 21% turn-on hours) and adapting to individual occupants.

The transcript also covers classification-vs-regression Q&A with attendees, cross-validation and confusion matrix (true positive/negative) discussion, and lunch-break logistics.

## Summarized attachments

- **ML algorithms** (`Lecture_Friday_2_Final.pdf`, PDF): Slide deck covering supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning algorithms with building-energy domain examples. Supervised learning: k-nearest neighbors (KNN) for earthquake vs nuclear-explosion seismic wave classification and adaptive thermal comfort modeling (Xiong & Yao 2021); overfitting/underfitting issues (low/high k trade-off); curse of dimensionality (degrades above ~15 dimensions); cross-validation for k selection. Decision trees with information gain (9-node example minimizing questions to 2), application to building energy demand prediction (Yu et al. 2010). Unsupervised learning: k-means clustering and elbow method for load-shape profiles (Park et al. 2019 building benchmarking study, k=3 chosen), bisecting k-means, DBSCAN. Reinforcement learning: Markov Decision Process state-action-reward formalization (car maintenance example), LightLearn occupancy-centered adaptive lighting controller learning occupant comfort preferences over 26 episodic states (occupancy, switch, light level, time-of-day), tested in 5 offices saving 82% vs 21% turn-on hours.
