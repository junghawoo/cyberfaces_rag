# Water, Food and Environmental Security

Springer book chapter examining water scarcity as critical sustainability challenge for global food production and environmental protection in the 21st century.

**Authors:** Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University. **Book:** "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Springer, Cham, 2016), Chapter 5, pp. 57-67. **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5. **ISBN:** 978-3-319-22661-3 (Print), 978-3-319-22662-0 (Online). **URL:** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_5

## Core Issues and Scale

Water scarcity identified as key issue for food and environmental sustainability. Agriculture represents largest global water consumer; irrigated agriculture accounts for 70% all freshwater withdrawals globally. Increasing portion sourced from groundwater, favored by farmers but facing emerging shortages with expected curtailed irrigation water supplies. Despite irrigated area comprising <20% global cropland, it produces ~40% food production. Shortages require significant rainfed area expansion to compensate production losses.

## Water Use Efficiency and Solutions

Considerable scope exists for increasing water use efficiency in agriculture through: improved delivery to plants; increased plant-level water use efficiency. Chapter explores water's role in agriculture and presents simulation results (SIMPLE model) highlighting interplay between irrigated agriculture restrictions and global land use change. Supplemental learning resources available at https://mygeohub.org/
