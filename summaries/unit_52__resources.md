# Resources

Springer book chapter "Population and Income as Drivers of Global Change" by Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos (Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University) from "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Springer, Cham, 2016; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_2; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_2; ISBN: 978-3-319-22661-3 print, 978-3-319-22662-0 online).

Chapter examines historical relationship between population and cropland expansion; role of per capita income growth in land use (agricultural and non-agricultural, including forests). Topics: population as determinant of future land use; demographic transition; population projections to 2050; population growth and regional consumption pattern differences driven by per capita incomes; income as determinant of consumption behavior; dietary upgrading (shift from starchy foods to meats, dairy, processed foods); consumer responses to food prices and income by income level; SIMPLE model projections to 2050 on global food demand and land use change implications.

Keywords: Population and food security, Income and dietary upgrading, Geographic variation in food consumption, Changing budget share of food, Demand drivers of global land use change. Supplemental resources available at https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change.

References include: Bloom on world population (Science, 2011); Clements & Chen on consumer behavior (Applied Economics, 1996); FAO food security statistics and FAOSTAT database (2012-2013); Fouré et al. on modeling world economy to 2050 (Economics of Transition, 2013); International Comparison Program (1996); Muhammad et al. on food consumption patterns using 2005 ICP data (USDA Economic Research Service, TB-1929); Reimer & Hertel on international demand estimates for GTAP model (Purdue Center for Global Trade Analysis); Roberts on population projections (Science, 2011); Southgate et al. on world food economy (2010); Thompson on population (American Journal of Sociology, 1929); UNPD World Population Prospects (2000, 2010, 2012 revisions); World Bank World Development Indicators (2013).

Related research: "Identifying and assessing online food delivery swamps in major Chinese cities" (2026); "Exploring association between socio-economic and environmental factors and food consumption in Iran using time series data" (2025); "The Distributional Impact of Climate Change: Why Food Prices Matter" (2021). Subject areas: Agricultural Economics, Applied Demography, Food Security, Population Economics, Population and Demography, Sustainable Growth.

GLASSNET Resources (https://mygeohub.org/groups/glassnet): Global research network addressing Sustainable Development Goals with limited land and water resources. Featured researcher: Alexandra Hill (UC Berkeley Assistant Professor) researching US agricultural workforce well-being and impacts on productivity, output quality, profits, workplace injuries. Featured event: Modelling Extremes seminar on climate change, biophysical impacts, extreme weather, agriculture, food security, adaptation, global value chains.
