# Biofuels as a Driver of Long Run Land Use Change

Springer book chapter examining biofuels' role in global land use change, market-mediated economic responses to feedstock demand, and implications for food and environmental security.

**Authors:** Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University. **Book:** "Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet" (Springer, Cham, 2016), Chapter 8, pp. 99-114. **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_8. **ISBN:** 978-3-319-22661-3 (Print), 978-3-319-22662-0 (Online). **Supplemental resources:** https://mygeohub.org/courses/global_change

## Biofuels Boom: Drivers and Impacts

**Overview:** Explores forces driving 'biofuel boom' and implications for food/environmental security. Bioenergy historically important (since fire); biofuels for transportation recent phenomenon. **Key impact:** Growing feedstock demand increased commodity prices, encouraging land conversion.

**Central question:** Examines 'market-mediated responses' to biofuels demand—how prices and economic behavior affect land conversion. **Quantitative analysis:** SIMPLE model simulations demonstrate early estimates overstated biofuels' impact on prices and land conversion when ignoring economic responses to scarcity.

## Regional and Policy Context

**Biofuels production globally:** US ethanol boom (Tyner, 2008), Brazil ethanol industry (Hira & de Oliveira, 2009), biofuels policy in Argentina, China, Indonesia, India, ASEAN countries. **EU Biofuel Directive 2009/30/EC:** establishes mandates and policies. **Policy impacts:** Government support varies (China subsidies tracked by Global Subsidies Initiative).

## Environmental and Food Security Implications

**GHG emissions:** Searchinger et al. (2008) documented US croplands conversion for biofuels increases greenhouse gases via land-use change. **Global land use:** Hertel et al. (2010) examined market-mediated land use impacts from US maize ethanol. **Food security nexus:** Competition for land between food and biofuel production (Hertel et al., 2013); Brown (2012) frames food scarcity as geopolitical issue.

## Research Methodologies and Tools

**SIMPLE model:** Computable general equilibrium model for global economic-agricultural simulations. **GEMPACK software:** Required for related modeling coursework; trial editions (Release 11.0+) with 6-month licenses available; RunDynam and RunGTAP programs support dynamic/GTAP model simulations.

## Key Research Contributors

Major scholars: Thomas Hertel, Wheat Tyner, Wallace Westhoff, Farzad Taheripour, Uris Baldos, Nelson Villoria. **Conferences:** 11th GTAP Conference (2008), American Agricultural Economics Association. **Organizations:** International Food Policy Research Institute (IFPRI), USDA Foreign Agricultural Service (country reports), NREL, International Energy Agency (World Energy Outlook), National Research Council.
