# Land-Based Environmental Services

This Foundation-level module bundles two external fetched resources: a Springer book chapter and the GEMPACK software installation guide.

## Book chapter (Springer Nature Link)
"Land-Based Environmental Services" (Chapter 7, pp. 85-98) by Thomas W. Hertel and Uris Lantz C. Baldos, Department of Agricultural Economics, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN. Published in *Global Change and the Challenges of Sustainably Feeding a Growing Planet*, Springer, Cham, 2016. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_7. Print ISBN 978-3-319-22661-3; Online ISBN 978-3-319-22662-0. eBook package: Earth and Environmental Science. URL: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_7.

Abstract themes: as the world becomes richer, competing priorities for land use emerge; developing countries set aside reserves to preserve biodiversity and enable eco-tourism. It reviews initiatives to reduce deforestation and forest degradation, including REDD+ initiatives tied to climate mitigation. The chapter examines environmental demands for land historically and in future, and implications for global cropland availability, with particular attention to land-based carbon sequestration as a climate change mitigation path. It uses SIMPLE model simulations in which land supply to agriculture is altered in light of projected carbon sequestration initiatives, highlighting impacts on food prices and food security. Notes reference a petagram (1.0E12 kg), the TIST project (tist.org), Eq. 7.1 for effective cropland supply shift (scalar 1.30), and Golub et al. (2013).

Keywords: Ecosystem services from land, Terrestrial carbon sequestration, REDD policies, Environmental competition for land, Food security and environment services. Related subjects: Ecosystem Services, Environmental Policy, Environmental Economics, Environmental Social Sciences, Food Security, Sustainability. Extensive reference list cites Aizen et al. (2009) on pollinators, Angelsen (2013/2014) on the economics of REDD+, Barbier & Tesfaw (2012), Busch et al. on Indonesian deforestation moratoriums, Coase (1960), De Koning et al. (2011) on Ecuador's Socio Bosque program, Golub et al. (2009, 2013), Muñoz-Piña et al. (2008) on Mexico's hydrological services, Nepstad et al. (2014, Amazon), Pagiola et al. (2005), Phalan et al. (2011) land sharing vs land sparing, Reilly et al. (2012), Ricketts et al. (2004) tropical forest coffee value, Sohngen (2010), Zhang et al. (2007), plus IEA, UN Population Division, Ecosystem Marketplace reports, and PBS Frontline "The Carbon Hunters."

## GEMPACK Software (installation guide)
URL: https://www.copsmodels.com/gplapsoft.htm. Instructions for installing GEMPACK on a laptop prior to a training course. Requires GEMPACK Release 11.0 or later (check version via Help...GEMPACK licence from TABmate or ViewHAR). Install requires Administrator access rights. Download and read quickinst.pdf (simplified install), then the Trial Edition of the Limited Executable-Image Version of GEMPACK (temporary 6-month licence); a longer document GPInstall.pdf exists but QuickInst Section 3 (running a model simulation to verify install) should be followed. Other course software mentioned: RunDynam (dynamic simulations) and RunGTAP (solves the GTAP model). References the MINIMAL free training software used in the Practical GE course (Melbourne). Notes on course CD/USB key, upgrading temporary licences (often to a 12-month course-specific licence), and laptop requirements.

## Summarized attachments
- **Book chapter: Land-Based Environmental Services** (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22662-0_7, URL): Springer book chapter by Hertel and Baldos examining competing land use priorities including biodiversity preservation, eco-tourism, REDD+ climate mitigation initiatives, and land-based carbon sequestration with SIMPLE model simulations showing impacts of carbon sequestration policy on cropland availability, food prices, and food security outcomes.
- **GEMPACK Software** (https://www.copsmodels.com/gplapsoft.htm, software tool): Computational General Equilibrium modeling software installation instructions requiring Release 11.0+ with pre-course Windows laptop setup, administrator access, quickinst.pdf instructions, Trial Edition with 6-month temporary license, and model simulation verification procedures for course completion.
